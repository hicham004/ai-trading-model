# Allocator v2 — the jointly sized portfolio with extra brakes (walk-forward, SIMULATION ONLY)

Plan row R5b (master plan Sprint 5, item 2). SIMULATION ONLY - research evidence for the paper fleet; nothing approved. Generated by `scripts/run_allocator_v2.py` from the Sprint 2c results (`docs/research/ensemble_v1c_results.json`) and the research data lake; numbers in `docs/research/allocator_v2_results.json`.

## In short (plain English)

- **What we tested.** The best Sprint 2c construction — six trend sleeves (S1, S2, S4 on BTC and ETH) sized together as one portfolio, with its volatility target and drawdown ladder — plus new brakes, each a multiplier between 0 and 1 on its own sizing: at most 50 % of the money in one bet (BTC and ETH count as one bet while they move together; checked at each day's close), and 7 days out of the market after losing 5 % in a week. The brake values were set before the study first ran and were not changed afterwards.
- **Same sleeves, same days.** Rebuilt from scratch, the old allocator reproduces Sprint 2c exactly (+10.9% a year, 13.2% worst fall), so the difference below is the brakes and nothing else.
- **Result, 4.4 years of walk-forward (2021-06-15 to 2025-10-20).** Allocator v2 made +10.5% a year with a 12.3% worst fall (Sharpe 0.86, Calmar 0.85), against +10.9% / 13.2% (Sharpe 0.82, Calmar 0.83) for v1. It passes every Sprint 2c gate.
- **How often the brakes acted.** The correlation cap trimmed the book on 5.5% of days; the weekly loss limit tripped once (0.23 a year), leaving the book in cash 0.4% of days; on average 15.8% of the money was invested (v1: 16.0%).
- **Untouched last 12 months** (opened because v2 passed, 2025-10-21 to 2026-10-19): **Both lost.** v2 -9.2% (worst fall 14.4%), v1 on the same sleeves -15.0% (15.8%), in a falling market (BTC -25.9%, ETH -39.5% for plain buy-and-hold). The brakes cut the loss but did not turn it into a gain. The holdout does not confirm v2's walk-forward pass, just as it did not confirm 3 of the 4 configurations Sprint 2c opened it for.
- **What it means.** 5 of the 6 long-only trend sleeves lost at full size on these months, and both portfolio constructions lost with them, more than every always-long control with the policy sizing (-4.3% to -2.6%). Both portfolio accounts keep running in the paper fleet (it costs nothing); on this evidence neither is a lead candidate, and the forward record decides.

## What allocator v2 adds

Allocator v1 (Sprint 2c, `research.portfolio.combine_sized`) is kept unchanged: the six sleeves run at FULL size; every day each gets a weight ∝ 1 / its trailing 60-day volatility; one portfolio volatility target of 15 % (trailing 30 days, never above 100 % = no leverage) scales the whole book; one drawdown ladder (half size at −7.5 %, flat at −15 %, 30 days flat then the peak is reset) acts on the portfolio's own equity. Allocator v2 (`research/allocator.py`) multiplies that sizing by brakes that are each between 0 and 1:

| Brake | Rule | Why this value |
|---|---|---|
| Correlation cap | Sleeves on the same coin are one bet; BTC and ETH are one bet while the correlation of their daily returns over the last 60 days is 0.70 or more (unknown = one bet). The money invested in one bet — the sleeves of the bet that were long at the previous close — is capped at 50 % of equity: above it the bet is scaled back to the cap. Sleeves that enter during a day count from the next close, so after several entries a bet can stand above the cap until then. | The 60-day window is v1's weight window; 0.70 is the conventional "highly correlated" line; one bet may hold at most half the book. |
| Weekly loss limit | A loss of 5 % or more over the last 7 days sends the book to cash. | A third of the 15 % annual volatility target, about 2.5 to 3 weekly standard deviations: a circuit breaker for bad weeks, not a routine brake. |
| Cooldown | After a trip, 7 days in cash, then the limit re-arms with a fresh 7-day window. The ladder keeps its own 30-day cooling-off. | Sit out the week after a bad week instead of jumping straight back in. |
| Vol target and ladder | Same rules as v1. The vol target is measured on the base weights, so a brake never raises the scale; the ladder follows v2's own equity. | The difference between v1 and v2 is then the new brakes and their effect on v2's own drawdown path. |

The values are `DEFAULT_ALLOCATOR_V2` in `research/allocator.py`. They were written before the study first ran in plan row R5b and were not changed after any result, but they were committed together with the results, so the repository itself cannot prove that order: the paper fleet's forward record is the test that cannot be tuned. "Only lowers" means relative to v2's own sizing: because v2's ladder follows v2's equity, it held v2 at a larger size than v1's ladder held v1 on 92 days (and the reverse on 0); v2 had more money invested than v1 on 68 days.

Everything a day's size depends on is known at the previous close (tests prove that changing a day cannot change its own size). Moving money between sleeves is assumed free, as in Sprint 2c; the cost of that re-weighting is estimated below.

## How often each brake acted (walk-forward)

- BTC/ETH correlation (trailing 60 days): median 0.84 (10th–90th percentile 0.71–0.92); the coins counted as one bet on 93.5% of days.
- Correlation cap: trimmed the book on 5.5% of days (2021: 5, 2022: 0, 2023: 22, 2024: 36, 2025: 24 days), by 22.8% on average when it did. v1 had more than 50 % of its equity in one bet at the previous close on 4.3% of days (at most 100.0%). On the days the cap acted, 5.1 of the six sleeves were long on average (2.4 on the other days, 2.1 ten days earlier) and the vol scale was 0.81 (0.69 otherwise): it acted when many sleeves had just entered together, before the trailing volatility had caught up.
- The cap is checked at the previous close, so it trims a bet one day after several sleeves entered together: a bet stood above 50 % at a day's close on 29 of 1589 days (at most 87.4%), and the next day's cap brought it back.
- Weekly loss limit: tripped once (2025-10-13); in cash after a trip on 0.4% of days.
- Drawdown ladder: v2 at half size on 50.5% and flat on 0.0% of days (v1: 56.3% / 0.0%).
- Invested money (start of each day): v2 15.8% on average, at most 68.8% (above 50 % only on days the coins counted as two bets); v1 16.0%, at most 100.0%. Both are low on average because the sleeves are flat much of the time and the ladder halves the book on about half of the days.
- Re-weighting between sleeves (assumed free, as in Sprint 2c): about 4.8 times the equity a year for v2 and 4.5 for v1, i.e. roughly 0.7% and 0.7% a year at 0.15 % per side if it were charged. A real book would re-weight only on larger changes.

## Gates (Sprint 2c portfolio gates, unchanged)

Profit factor above 1.2 (pooled), max drawdown below 20 %, Sharpe above 0.7, Calmar above 0.5, at least 75 % of rolling 12-month windows positive and no calendar year below −10 %, at least 30 trades (pooled); the ±30 % robustness gate is carried by the sleeves, as in Sprint 2c. Verdict for v2: **none — PASS**. No gate was changed after the results were in.

**Do the gates look wrong?** Not one by one, but they are too easy for a portfolio, and that is worth fixing before the next portfolio study (as a new, pre-registered rule, not as an edit of this one): at the portfolio level the ±30 % robustness gate is skipped; the profit factor and trade count are pooled from sleeves of which 0 of 6 passed on their own; the sleeves' timeframes were picked on out-of-sample numbers; 88 % of v1's walk-forward growth came from its two best calendar years (2023 and 2024). A portfolio cleared every gate and was not confirmed by its holdout. Recommendation for future studies: count a portfolio as a candidate only when its sleeves pass on their own or its holdout confirms it.

## What happens next

- The paper fleet runs allocator v2 as its own account, `pf-s1s2s4-btceth-vt15-alloc2`, on the same six sleeves as the v1 portfolio account `pf-s1s2s4-btceth-vt15` (which is unchanged). Its journal also carries the v1 rules on its own sleeves, so the weekly report shows the brakes' own effect day by day even if the two accounts started at different times. The walk-forward numbers above are its expectations, and its holdout result is a second reference, as for the shortlist accounts.
- The weekly report's allocator section compares the two and lists every brake that acted. A good forward record is evidence for plan row G7, not an approval.
- Allocator v2 is research code (`research/`). The demo and live risk manager (`app/risk`) stays the reviewed core; moving any of these brakes there is a separate, reviewed owner decision.

## Method notes and caveats

- The sleeves are Sprint 2c's: per family and coin the timeframe with the higher walk-forward Calmar (a mild selection on out-of-sample data, already flagged in Sprint 2c), the rule each sleeve's walk-forward selected most often, replayed at full size in the same 180-day test windows, each window starting flat. Portfolio numbers inherit that mild optimism, for v1 and v2 alike.
- A sleeve counts as invested when it was long at the previous day's close (its last bar of the day); a sleeve that enters during the day joins at the weight it was given that morning (see the cap's numbers above).
- Coin correlations use one close per UTC day from the 1H candles, from the start of the coin history (so the first walk-forward days already have a 60-day correlation); a missing close leaves no return across the gap.
- The holdout was computed more than once in this step: first to open it, then again each time statistics or fixes were added to the study (one fix moved its first booked day to the first full holdout day). The brake configuration never changed, and every number here is from the final code.
- The study reproduces Sprint 2c only on the same data: the 12-month split follows the lake's last bar (the last bars used are recorded in the results file). After the lake grows, the reproduction check stops a re-run instead of comparing different samples.
- Every number is a simulation on historical public data and proves nothing about the future.
